# The Park in 2001

*Figures from the 2001 Census community profile for The Park, produced by Policy and
Information, City Development, Nottingham City Council, 11 March 2004. Source: 2001 Census,
Key Statistics. Crown copyright 2003, reproduced with the permission of the Controller of
HMSO. "City" below means Nottingham as a whole.*

The record runs from 1861 to 1939. This is what the estate had become sixty years later, and
it is worth holding beside the census rounds because it answers questions the rounds raise.

---

## The long arc

| | people | households | average household |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1881 | 1,774 | 199 | **8.91** |
| 1911 | 1,424 | 249 | 5.72 |
| 1921 | 1,503 | 296 | 5.08 |
| 1939 | 1,369 | 341 | 4.01 |
| **2001** | **2,272** | **1,261** | **1.80** |

*(1861 to 1939 are counts from this record and will move as transcription continues. 2001 is
the census profile.)*

**The households multiplied and the houses did not.** Between 1939 and 2001 the population
rose by two thirds while the number of households nearly quadrupled. The estate's 442
properties had become **1,301 dwellings** - the big houses divided into flats, which is the
single largest physical change in the estate's history and the reason so much of this record's
work goes into houses carrying two and three and five schedule numbers.

**Four in ten households in 2001 were one person living alone** - 514 of them, against a city
figure of 23 per cent. Another 118 were a single pensioner. In 1881 the average house held
nearly nine people.

**Service is the difference.** Of the 1,369 people this record holds for 1939, **622 - very
nearly half - are returned in domestic service**: cooks, housemaids, parlourmaids,
housekeepers, nurses, chauffeurs and gardeners. Those people are the missing half of the
arithmetic above. The houses did not empty; the servants left.

---

## Empty houses

**In 2001, 60 of the 1,301 dwellings stood vacant - 4.6 per cent.**

The record currently marks **101 Park houses ticked and left blank on the night of 29
September 1939**, against 341 with a household: **a little under a quarter of the estate
standing empty three weeks after war was declared.** That figure will move as the round is
completed, and some of it is evacuation rather than abandonment, but the contrast with 2001
is the right way round and large.

---

## Who lived here

**It was still a wealthy estate.** In 2001:

| | The Park | city |
|---|---|---|
| Large employers and higher managerial | **8.8%** | 2.0% |
| Higher professional | **24.1%** | 4.2% |
| Lower managerial and professional | 25.3% | 13.2% |
| Routine and semi-routine | 4.7% | 23.1% |
| Qualified to degree level or above | **60.9%** | 17.6% |
| Self-employed | 13.3% | 4.6% |

**And a rented one.** 33.8 per cent of households rented from a private landlord against a
city figure of 13.1 - the divided houses again - and only 3.7 per cent from the council,
against 26.9.

**It walks to work.** A quarter of working residents went on foot (city 14.7%) and another
10.8 per cent worked at home. The estate sits against the city centre, which is why the 1939
Register finds clerks, shop assistants and civil servants living beside the lace
manufacturers.

**Cars.** Only a fifth of households had none, against nearly half across the city; three in
ten had two or more. The estate was laid out for carriages and its stables and coach houses
are the buildings this record most often cannot find - Morisco, Barbican House, Stuart
Cottage, Palisades.

---

## A detail that bears on the record

**The Park's Jewish population in 2001 was 1.5 per cent against a city figure of 0.23 - more
than six times the Nottingham rate**, and the highest of any minority religion on the estate
relative to the city.

That is not a new thing. The households this record holds include **Hesse** at Stowe House,
**Weinberg** at Hardwicke House, **Goldschmid**, **Raflowitch** at 9 Cavendish Crescent North,
**Likiernik** at Adam House, **Hirschfield** at Felixstowe and others. A continuity of a
century is worth noticing and worth writing up properly.

---

## Health and age

- **79.0 per cent** described their health as good, against 65.0 across the city.
- **9.4 per cent** had a limiting long-term illness or disability, against 20.1.
- **41.6 per cent** of residents were aged 25 to 44, against 29.2 across the city; only 8.4
  per cent were under 16, against 19.8. A young, working, childless estate.
